Benedito José Tobias was a São Paulo–born painter and draftsman active primarily in the 1930s and 1940s, recognized as one of the first Black artists to gain prominence in the city's visual arts scene. He worked across landscape, still life, and urban genre painting, but is best known for small-scale portraits of Black men and women rendered in oil on wood and gouache or watercolor on paper, attentive to the physical features and social condition of his sitters.

His paintings entered major Brazilian institutional collections, including the Museu Afro Brasil, MASP, and the Pinacoteca do Estado de São Paulo, which holds the urban scene *Gigantes e Pigmeus*. Renewed scholarly attention followed his inclusion in the 2008 *Negros Pintores* exhibition at the Museu Afro Brasil, and his work has since been the subject of academic study and a 2022 MASP lecture on Black authorship in portraiture.
