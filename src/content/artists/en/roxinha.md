Roxinha — the artistic name of Maria José Lisboa da Cruz — was born in 1953 in the settlement of Lagoa de Pedra, in Pão de Açúcar, in the backlands of Alagoas, on the banks of the São Francisco River. Before art, her life was one of manual labor: from adolescence she farmed cassava, beans and corn, broke rocks in a quarry, and worked as a street sweeper for almost two decades in Lagoa de Pedra, where she still lives. "The stones I used to break, today I paint," she likes to say.

In her maturity she began to draw as a pastime alongside her husband, Domingos, to fill her time and her longing for her grown children. From 2021 she started painting on pieces of MDF, wood and materials found in vacant lots, in vivid colors and with short, good-humored captions. Her work portrays rural daily life and the magical universe of television soap operas, with their couples and love stories. Discovered by curator André Dantas, her paintings covered the walls of his cultural space on Ilha do Ferro (Alagoas), and in 2023 she had her first solo exhibition — "Roxinha: uma vida de novela" (Roxinha: A Life of Soap Opera) — at the Museu do Pontal in Rio de Janeiro, with 70 paintings.
