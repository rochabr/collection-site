Roberto de Oliveira Magalhães is a Brazilian painter, draftsman, and printmaker who emerged in Rio de Janeiro in the early 1960s, holding his first solo show of drawings at Galeria Macunaíma at the Escola Nacional de Belas Artes in 1962 and taking up engraving at ENBA the following year. Aligned with the new figuration that surfaced in *Opinião 65* (MAM-RJ, 1965) and *Nova Objetividade Brasileira* (MAM-RJ, 1967), he built an independent trajectory alongside contemporaries such as Antonio Dias, Carlos Vergara, and Rubens Gerchman.

His woodcut production of 1963–1966 is characterized by stark black-and-white compositions, occasionally punctuated with red, depicting portraits, self-portraits, wheels suggesting movement, and battle scenes inflected with dark humor. He received the engraving prize at the IV Paris Biennial in 1965 and exhibited internationally, including *Brazilian Art Today* at the Royal Academy of Arts in London (1964); in 1992 the Centro Cultural Banco do Brasil in Rio mounted a retrospective of his first thirty years of production.
