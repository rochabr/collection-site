Ivan Serpa was a Rio de Janeiro painter active from the late 1940s until his death in 1973, who became a central figure in the development of geometric abstraction and Concrete art in Brazil. He received the Young National Painter Prize at the 1st São Paulo Bienal in 1951, and in 1954 founded Grupo Frente alongside artists including Lygia Clark, Lygia Pape, Hélio Oiticica, Franz Weissmann, and Abraham Palatnik.

Beginning in 1952, he taught children and adults at the Museu de Arte Moderna do Rio de Janeiro for two decades, training a generation that included Oiticica, Pape, Aluísio Carvão, Antonio Manuel, and Waltercio Caldas. Working across oil painting, collage, and drawing, he moved through rigorous geometric construction, a gestural "Crepuscular" (or "black") phase in the early 1960s, and a return to geometric form in 1965 — a foundational practitioner of Brazilian constructive art who also shaped much of Rio's neo-concrete and post-concrete generation.
