José Soares da Silva (1937–2019), known as Mestre Dila, was a Brazilian cordel poet and woodcut printmaker (xilógrafo) from the Agreste region of Pernambuco. Born in Bom Jardim, he settled in Caruaru around 1952, where he worked in local print shops producing engravings for newspapers, chapbook covers, labels, and other commercial printing. He went on to write, publish, and illustrate his own cordel booklets, and produced woodcuts for other poets, including J. Borges.

Working from his Caruaru workshop, Dila carved matrices not only in wood but also in vulcanized rubber, a technique uncommon among popular printmakers, and developed multicolor printing methods for his folhetos and prints. His imagery drew on the imagination of the Northeastern backlands: the exploits of the cangaceiro Lampião, the figure of the Devil, and the miracles of Padre Cícero, blending reality and fantasy. Author of more than 200 cordéis, he was named a Living Heritage of Pernambuco (Patrimônio Vivo) in 2002 and is regarded as a master of the genre.
