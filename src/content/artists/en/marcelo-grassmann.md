Marcelo Grassmann was a Brazilian printmaker, draftsman, and illustrator born in São Simão (SP) in 1925. He studied at Liceu de Artes e Ofícios de São Paulo from 1942 and, in 1952, was awarded a scholarship to study engraving in Vienna at the Akademie der Bildenden Künste. He is recognized as one of the most important Brazilian printmakers of the twentieth century.

His work engages medieval and Renaissance traditions — Dürer, Bosch, Goya — exploring a fantastic and grotesque imaginary: armored horsemen, hybrid figures, fabulous animals, religious processions, and battle scenes. His preferred technique was metal engraving (etching, drypoint, aquatint), worked with graphic density and dramatic light.

He received major prizes across his career, including acquisition prizes at several editions of the Bienal de São Paulo and the International Prize at the Tokyo International Print Biennale (1979). His works are held in the collections of MAM-SP, the Pinacoteca do Estado de São Paulo, MoMA (New York), and the Victoria & Albert Museum (London). He died in São Paulo in 2013.
