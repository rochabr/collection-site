Athos Bulcão was a Rio-born painter, printmaker, and muralist whose career, from the late 1940s onward, became inseparable from the construction of modern Brasília. After early work in illustration at the Ministry of Education and Culture and a period dedicated to photomontage in the 1950s, he joined Novacap in 1957 and began a long collaboration with Oscar Niemeyer, producing the tile panels, reliefs, and stained glass that mark such landmarks as the Palácio do Itamaraty, the Church of Our Lady of Fátima, the Brasília Palace Hotel, and the city's airport.

His signature azulejo work uses modular geometric units recombined into non-repeating compositions, integrating painting and architecture in a distinctively Brazilian modernist key. Alongside the architectural commissions he sustained an independent practice in painting and printmaking, and from the 1970s extended the architectural-integration work with João Filgueiras Lima (Lelé) on the Sarah Kubitschek hospital network. The Fundação Athos Bulcão, established in Brasília in 1993, preserves his archive, catalogues his works, and administers his estate.
