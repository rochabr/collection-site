Djanira da Motta e Silva (Avaré, São Paulo, 1914 – Rio de Janeiro, 1979) was a Brazilian painter, draughtswoman, and printmaker, and one of the significant figures of Brazilian modernism. Of modest, rural background, she spent her childhood and youth in Avaré and in Porto União, Santa Catarina. She was largely self-taught: after being treated for tuberculosis in a sanatorium, where she made her first drawings, she settled in Rio de Janeiro in 1939 and opened a boarding house in the Santa Teresa neighbourhood. There she lived among modernist artists such as Milton Dacosta, Carlos Scliar, Maria Helena Vieira da Silva, and Arpad Szenes, and attended drawing classes at the Liceu de Artes e Ofícios.

From 1945 she lived for several years in New York, encountering the work of Bruegel, Miró, and Chagall. Returning to Brazil, she turned to popular and social subjects — coffee pickers, fishermen, weavers, factory and mill workers — alongside portraits, landscapes, and religious scenes, and produced large tile panels such as *Santa Bárbara* in Rio de Janeiro. Her work is held by major institutions including MASP and the Pinacoteca de São Paulo; in 2019 MASP presented the retrospective "Djanira: a memória do seu povo."
