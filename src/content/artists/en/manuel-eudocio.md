Manuel Eudócio Rodrigues (1931–2016) was a Brazilian ceramist born and raised in Alto do Moura, the artisan district of Caruaru, Pernambuco. He came from a family of potters and learned to model clay as a child alongside his maternal grandmother, who made utilitarian wares and clay dolls. In 1948 he began working in figurative sculpture after Mestre Vitalino moved to Alto do Moura, becoming one of the last surviving artisans of Vitalino's generation and helping establish the community as a leading center of figurative clay art in the Americas.

Working in hand-modeled, kiln-fired clay finished with paint, Eudócio refused to use molds and built an extensive repertoire of subjects drawn from Northeastern life and folklore: cangaceiros, rural weddings, musicians and bands, the bumba-meu-boi, the reisado, and the bull that became his emblem. Over a career of more than eight decades he produced tens of thousands of pieces. He was designated a Patrimônio Vivo (Living Heritage) of Pernambuco under state law no. 12.196 of 2 May 2002.
