Denilson Monteiro Baniwa was born in 1984 in Darí, a village in the Barcelos region of the Amazonian Rio Negro — Baniwa territory, in the tri-border area between Brazil, Colombia and Venezuela. He is a visual artist, curator, designer, and activist, and one of the most influential voices in contemporary Indigenous Brazilian art.

His practice cuts across painting, collage, video, installation, performance, and digital media. The work braids Baniwa cosmology and upper Rio Negro iconography with references drawn from pop culture, cinema, and the canon of Western art history — operating, as the artist puts it, as a "right of reply" to the colonial fiction that has structured how Indigenous people were represented in Brazil. He was one of the curators of the Brazilian pavilion at the 2024 Venice Biennale (Hãhãwpuá / "Foreign Land") and his work has been shown at MASP, the São Paulo Biennial, the Sydney Biennial, the Princeton University Art Museum, and the Getty, among others.
