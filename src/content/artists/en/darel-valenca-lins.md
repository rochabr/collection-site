Darel Valença Lins, known simply as Darel, was a Brazilian painter, draftsman, engraver, illustrator, and professor born in Palmares (PE) in 1924 and died in Rio de Janeiro in 2017. He began with technical drawing in 1937 and studied at the Recife School of Fine Arts between 1941 and 1942. In 1946 he moved to Rio de Janeiro, where he refined metal engraving at the Liceu de Artes e Ofícios in 1948.

He received the bronze medal at the 54th Salão Nacional de Belas Artes (1948) and the engraving prize at the 1st Salão de Arte Moderna do Recife (1952). In 1957 he won the travel-abroad prize from the Salão Nacional de Arte Moderna and lived in Italy until 1960. He taught at MASP (1951), the Escola Nacional de Belas Artes (1955-1957), and FAAP (1961-1964), and worked for decades as an illustrator for Editora José Olympio, *Manchete* magazine, and the newspapers *Última Hora* and *Diário de Notícias*. In 1982 he received the Prêmio Abril de Jornalismo for his illustrations in *Playboy* magazine.

His work is marked by the exploration of chiaroscuro and full-versus-empty contrasts, around two recurrent themes: *imaginary cities* — dense architectural labyrinths in perspective — and the *angels and machines* series. He worked in engraving, lithography, drawing, watercolor, painting, collage, and photomontage. *Um dia Comum*, in the collection, is a large-format pastel on paper (60 × 80 cm) representative of his urban-figurative register.
