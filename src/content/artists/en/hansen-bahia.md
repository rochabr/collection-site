Karl Heinz Hansen, known as Hansen Bahia, was born in Hamburg (Germany) in 1915 and died in São Félix (BA) in 1978. He served in the German Navy during World War II and began his artistic work in Stockholm in 1946 with illustrations and woodcuts. He emigrated to Brazil in 1950, initially settling in São Paulo, where he worked for Companhia Melhoramentos until 1955.

In 1955 he moved to Salvador, drawn by the flourishing Bahian modernist movement, and formed friendships with Carybé, Jorge Amado, José Pedreira, and Carlos Eduardo da Rocha. He naturalized as Brazilian in 1966 and adopted the epithet *Bahia*, summarized in the phrase attributed to him: *"Everything I know and everything I am, I owe to Bahia."* From 1963 to 1966 he directed a course in metal engraving and woodcut in Addis Ababa (Ethiopia); back in Brazil, he offered free woodcut courses at the Brazil-Germany Cultural Institute.

His work reached maturity in woodcut — thick expressive lines in the 1950s and finer filigree work in the 1960s and 1970s — with themes concentrated on the urban life of Salvador's historic center, the Recôncavo region, and illustrations for literary classics (Castro Alves, Bertolt Brecht, François Villon). Before his death he donated his artistic production to the city of Cachoeira, where the Hansen Bahia Foundation was established. *Mãe e Filha* (1954), in the collection, is a woodcut from his early Bahian period, in an edition of just ten impressions.
