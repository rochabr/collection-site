Carybé (Hector Julio Páride Bernabó) was born in Lanús, Argentina, in 1911 and moved with his family to Brazil as a child. He trained at the National School of Fine Arts in Rio de Janeiro (1927–1929) and worked early on as an illustrator and journalist. After first travelling to Salvador in the mid-1940s, he settled permanently in Bahia in 1950, where he lived until his death in 1997.

In Bahia he devoted his work to Afro-Brazilian culture, capoeira, popular religiosity, and the orixás of Candomblé, working in painting, drawing, engraving, illustration, ceramics, mosaic, and large architectural panels. He published *Iconografia dos Deuses Africanos no Candomblé da Bahia* (1981) after some thirty years of research, illustrated books by Jorge Amado and others, and held the honorary title of Obá de Xangô at the Ilê Axé Opô Afonjá terreiro. His twenty-seven carved cedar panels of orixás, commissioned in 1968, are now held by the Museu Afro Brasil in São Paulo.
