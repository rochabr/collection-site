Tadashi Kaminagai (Hiroshima, Japan, 1899 — Paris, France, 1982) was a painter, draftsman, and teacher. At his family's initiative he entered a Buddhist monastery in Kobe at the age of 14. Two years later he left for the Dutch East Indies, present-day Indonesia, where he worked as a missionary and farmer until 1927.

That year, having decided on an artistic career, he moved to Paris, where he met Tsugouharu Foujita (1886–1968), who guided him in painting. Alongside his own work he earned his living as a framer. In the early 1930s he exhibited in the Paris salons — the Salon d'Automne at the Grand Palais, the Salon des Tuileries, and the Salon National des Beaux-Arts — and returned to Japan in 1938.

He sailed for Brazil in 1940, a year after the outbreak of the Second World War, carrying a letter of introduction to Candido Portinari (1903–1962). He settled in Rio de Janeiro and in 1941 opened a studio and frame workshop in Santa Teresa, where he worked and taught a number of Brazilian and Japanese-Brazilian artists, among them Inimá de Paula (1918–1999), Flavio-Shiró (1928), and Tikashi Fukushima (1920–2001). His first solo exhibition, around 1945, was organized by Portinari and held at the Hotel Serrador in Rio de Janeiro. In 1947 he joined Grupo Seibi.

In Brazil he received a silver medal at the 50th Salão Nacional de Belas Artes (1944) and took part in the 1st and 2nd São Paulo International Biennial (1951 and 1953) and the 1st Salão Nacional de Arte Moderna at MAM/RJ (1952). He returned to Japan in 1954 and, three years later, settled again in Paris. He lived between Japan, France, and Brazil until his death in 1982.
