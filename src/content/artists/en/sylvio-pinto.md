Sylvio Pinto, the name under which Sílvio Pinto da Silva signed his work (also as S. Pinto), was a painter, set designer, and teacher born in Rio de Janeiro on March 17, 1918. The son of Portuguese painter Bernardo Pinto da Silva — known as "Pinto das Tintas," his first master — he trained in his father's studio and at the Liceu de Artes e Ofícios in Rio de Janeiro. In the early 1930s he joined the Núcleo Bernardelli, where he worked alongside José Pancetti, Manuel Santiago, Bustamante Sá, Milton Dacosta, and Ado Malagoli, a milieu decisive to his formation.

From 1939 he produced theatrical sets and floats for Rio's carnival, and in 1940 he founded a free art school for poor children in the Jacarezinho neighborhood. He took part in the 1st São Paulo Biennial in 1951, and in 1952 won the travel-abroad prize at the Salão Nacional de Belas Artes — between 1953 and 1954 he passed through Lisbon, Madrid, and Seville before settling in Paris, the period of some of his finest urban landscapes. In 1977 he traveled to the United States and, in 1981, set up a studio in Ellenville, New York.

Though best known as a marine painter — often associated with Pancetti through their shared sea themes — Sylvio Pinto devoted a significant part of his work to popular subjects: fishermen, circuses, favelas, markets, festivals, and portraits of young girls, always treating the human figure with solemn respect. He received the Pedro Ernesto medal (1988), the Order of Merit medal from Rio's Legislative Assembly (1989), and honors in Portugal (1991) and Brasília (1993). In 1993 the Museu Nacional de Belas Artes opened the retrospective *Sylvio Pinto — 75 Years / 55 of Painting*. He died in Rio de Janeiro on April 3, 1997.
