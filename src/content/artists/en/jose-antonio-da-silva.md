Self-taught painter, draftsman, sculptor and writer, José Antônio da Silva worked as a rural laborer in the interior of São Paulo before being discovered in 1946 at an exhibition at the Casa de Cultura in São José do Rio Preto, where critics Lourival Gomes Machado and Paulo Mendes de Almeida championed his work. He participated in the 1st Bienal Internacional de São Paulo in 1951, returned to several later editions, and was given a special room at the 33rd Venice Biennale in 1966.

His paintings depict rural São Paulo — cotton and coffee fields, small-town fairs, storms, religious scenes — in a vivid, high-color idiom often compared to Van Gogh, and he extended his practice into novels, poetry, and recordings of folk verse. In 1980 the Museu de Arte Primitivista José Antônio da Silva was founded in São José do Rio Preto.
