Ivan João Alvarez Marquetti was a painter, draftsman, and printmaker from Rio de Janeiro, active between the 1960s and the 2000s, best known for his urban landscapes of the historic towns of Minas Gerais. 

He studied engraving and drawing at the Instituto de Belas Artes da Guanabara from 1958 to 1961 and, in 1962, settled in Ouro Preto, where he lived for much of his life and grew close to artists such as Carlos Scliar, Inimá de Paula, Vinícius de Moraes, and Carlos Bracher. He also worked in still life and flowers, with a distinctive style that fused the vibrant palette of Fauvism with an expressionism of straight lines and bold textures applied with a palette knife.

After honing his techniques in Paris between 1963 and 1964, he divided his time among Cabo Frio, Rio de Janeiro, Olinda, Ouro Preto, and Diamantina. In 1967 he founded the Serigraphy Group (Grupo de Serigrafia) in Rio de Janeiro and, from 1971 onward, held solo exhibitions in capitals such as Recife, Brasília, São Paulo, and Rio de Janeiro. Between 1979 and 1981, he traveled up the Rio Negro in search of inspiration, bringing back from that journey a series of works depicting the forests and rivers of the Amazon.
