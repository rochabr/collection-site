Fulvio Pennacchi (Villa Collemandina, Italy, 1905 – São Paulo, Brazil, 1992) trained at the Royal Academy of Fine Arts in Lucca, where he graduated in mural painting in 1928, with further study in Florence and Pisa. He emigrated to Brazil in 1929 and settled in São Paulo, where he remained for the rest of his life. In the 1930s he joined the Grupo Santa Helena alongside Alfredo Volpi, Francisco Rebolo, Aldo Bonadei and Mario Zanini, and took part in the Família Artística Paulista.

Pennacchi worked in oil, fresco, drawing and ceramics. He is associated with the revival of fresco and mural painting in São Paulo, executing panels for churches, public buildings and private homes, including a fresco cycle for the Igreja Nossa Senhora da Paz. His easel painting combines references to Italian early-Renaissance art with modern figuration, treating religious subjects and scenes of rural Brazilian ("caipira") life. A 1973 retrospective at the Museu de Arte de São Paulo (MASP) consolidated his recognition, and his work is held in Brazilian collections including the Museu de Arte Contemporânea da USP (MAC/USP), to which he donated a body of drawings.
