Severino Pereira dos Santos (1940–2019), known as Severino Vitalino, was a Brazilian ceramist born in the rural locality of Sítio Campos, near Caruaru, Pernambuco. He was a son of the ceramist Mestre Vitalino (Vitalino Pereira dos Santos) and Joana Maria da Conceição, and as a child moved with his family to the Alto do Moura neighborhood of Caruaru. 

He learned to model clay directly from his father, helping to shape small figures from a very young age, and throughout his life he deliberately preserved his father's style and repertoire — the fife band, the family of migrants (retirantes), the ox, and the cangaceiros Lampião and Maria Bonita, among other recurring scenes of Northeastern life.

After his father's death in 1963, Severino became the principal keeper of that legacy. He directed the Casa Museu Mestre Vitalino, installed in the family home in Alto do Moura, and continued producing figures in the inherited repertoire — long regarded as the son who remained most active in the craft. His large family of children and grandchildren, many of whom also took up ceramics, extended the Vitalino lineage of artisans in the neighborhood. He died in Caruaru in January 2019.
