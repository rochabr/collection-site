Born in Vienna in 1912, Lothar Charoux emigrated to Brazil in 1928 and trained at the Liceu de Artes e Ofícios in São Paulo, beginning his career with landscapes and portraits before moving toward geometric abstraction. In 1952 he was one of the seven founding signatories of the *Ruptura* manifesto, launched alongside Waldemar Cordeiro, Geraldo de Barros, Luiz Sacilotto, Kazmer Féjer, Anatol Władysław, and Leopoldo Haar at the Museu de Arte Moderna de São Paulo — the event that marked the formal start of Concrete art in Brazil.

His mature work is defined by precise line studies and modular geometric compositions that investigate two-dimensional space, optical vibration, and kinetic effect. He was also a founding member of the Associação de Artes Visuais Novas Tendências and remains a central reference for the São Paulo Concrete movement.
