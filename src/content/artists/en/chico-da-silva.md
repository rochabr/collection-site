Francisco Domingos da Silva moved from the Acre Amazon to Fortaleza as a child and was, by the 1940s, drawing animals and fantastical figures in charcoal and chalk on the walls of the Pirambu neighborhood, where the Swiss painter Jean-Pierre Chabloz encountered his work and encouraged him to take up gouache and paint. Working largely self-taught, he built a singular pictorial universe of dragons, fish, serpents, roosters, mermaids, and winged hybrids set against densely pointillist, high-chroma backgrounds drawn from Amazonian and Northeastern imaginaries.

From the early 1960s he organized the Ateliê do Pirambu, a collective studio of younger painters that produced large-scale works under his direction. He received an honorable mention at the 33rd Venice Biennale in 1966, the recognition that fixed his international reputation, and later exhibited at the São Paulo Biennial in 1967. He remains one of the central figures of twentieth-century Cearense painting, with major holdings at the Museu de Arte da UFC (MAUC) in Fortaleza.
