Niobe Xandó (Campos Novos Paulista, São Paulo, 1915 – São Paulo, 2010) was a Brazilian painter, draftsperson, and writer. Largely self-taught, she moved to the city of São Paulo in 1932 and began working as an artist in 1947, frequenting the circle of painters such as Yoshiya Takaoka and Geraldo de Barros and holding her first solo exhibition in 1953 at the Livraria das Bandeiras in São Paulo.

Her output moved from figurative scenes toward abstraction, passing through fantastic floral compositions and the "Máscaras" (Masks) series inspired by ancestral and Afro-Brazilian sources, and later toward grafismos and letrismo — abstraction built from signs and letters — as well as experimental works she termed "mecanismo." She showed at the São Paulo Bienal and in Europe, including Paris and Madrid. Her work is held in Brazilian institutions such as the Pinacoteca do Estado de São Paulo, MAC-USP, and MAM-SP, and is documented by the Enciclopédia Itaú Cultural.
