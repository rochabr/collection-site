Abraham Palatnik was a Brazilian painter, sculptor, designer, and inventor born in Natal (RN) in 1928. In 1932 he emigrated with his family to Palestine, where he studied technical drawing and mechanics in Tel Aviv before returning to Brazil in 1948. In 1953 he joined Grupo Frente in Rio de Janeiro alongside Ivan Serpa, Aluísio Carvão, and Lygia Clark, and is considered the pioneer of kinetic art in Brazil.

His most decisive contribution is the *Aparelhos Cinecromáticos* — electromechanical machines that project light and color in continuous motion, first shown at the São Paulo Biennial of 1951. From the 1960s onward he developed the *Progressive Reliefs* and *Kinetic Objects* series, and in parallel maintained a large-scale production of small figurative objects in acrylic resin — animals, dolls, and decorative forms — conceived in his atelier and widely circulated on the Brazilian secondary market.

His works are held in the collections of MAM-RJ, the Pinacoteca do Estado de São Paulo, MoMA (New York), and Tate Modern (London). He received a special prize at the São Paulo Biennial of 1951 and was the subject of retrospectives at MAM-Rio (1999) and the Pinacoteca de São Paulo. He died in Rio de Janeiro in 2020.
