Judith Lauand trained at the School of Fine Arts of Araraquara and moved to São Paulo in 1952, where she shifted from figuration to geometric abstraction in the mid-1950s. In 1955 she was invited by Waldemar Cordeiro to join Grupo Ruptura, becoming the only woman to actively participate in Brazil's Concrete movement, and she took part in the 1st National Exhibition of Concrete Art (MAM-SP 1956, MAM-RJ 1957) after serving as a monitor at the 2nd Bienal de São Paulo in 1954.

Her practice combined mathematical rigor with rhythm and dynamic balance across painting, drawing, collage, and works incorporating staples, clips and thumbtacks, later opening to looser geometric, experimental, and pop-inflected directions. MASP staged the largest retrospective of her work, *Judith Lauand: Concrete Detour*, in 2022–2023 with 128 works spanning six decades, consolidating her position as a central yet long-overlooked figure of Brazilian constructivism.
