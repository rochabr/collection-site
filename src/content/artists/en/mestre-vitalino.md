Vitalino Pereira dos Santos (1909–1963), known as Mestre Vitalino, was a Brazilian ceramist born in the rural area of Caruaru, Pernambuco. The son of farmers, he began as a child modeling small animals from leftover clay used by his mother to make household pottery sold at the Caruaru market. He later developed a body of figurative clay sculpture depicting the daily life, labor, religion, festivals, and folklore of the Northeastern backlands, including recurrent scenes of order and banditry such as the cangaceiros Lampião, Maria Bonita, and Corisco.

His work, previously little known, was presented in Rio de Janeiro in 1947 through the Exhibition of Pernambuco Popular Ceramics organized by Augusto Rodrigues, followed by a 1949 showing at the Museu de Arte de São Paulo (MASP). His figures established an iconographic model that shaped later generations of artisans in the Alto do Moura neighborhood of Caruaru. His former residence became the Casa Museu Mestre Vitalino, and his work is held in Brazilian and international collections.
