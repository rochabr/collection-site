Manoel Araújo, known as Manezinho Araújo, was a singer, composer, journalist, and painter born in Cabo de Santo Agostinho (PE) in 1913. Acclaimed as *the King of the Embolada*, he dedicated himself to music from the 1930s onward, recording roughly a hundred *emboladas* for labels including Odeon and Continental and building one of the central careers in Northeastern Brazilian popular music.

He began painting in 1950, self-taught. In 1957, by then living in Rio de Janeiro, he ended his musical career and devoted himself simultaneously to painting and to the Northeastern restaurant he opened in the city. His first solo exhibition took place in 1963 at Galeria Astréia in São Paulo. In 1968 he published in Rio the album of serigraphs *Meu Brasil*, presented by Aldemir Martins.

His painting is a landmark of Brazilian naïf art: oil on canvas in vibrant palette, frontal and narrative compositions — beach scenes, fishing villages, markets, popular festivities, and Northeastern processions, peopled with small narrative figures. *Casas de Pescadores* (1967), in the collection, exemplifies this register. He died in São Paulo in 1993.
