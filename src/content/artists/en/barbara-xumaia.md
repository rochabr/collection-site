Barbara Xumaia, the artistic name of Bárbara Elizabeth Schulmayr (Guzzi), is a painter and printmaker born in Berlin, Germany, on July 7, 1936. She moved to Brazil with her parents in 1955, while still young, and built her entire artistic career here, becoming one of the most recognizable voices in Brazilian naïve painting. Her encounter with the country — its light, its festivals, and the simplicity of everyday popular life — shaped the subject matter she would devote her whole body of work to.

Her talent for painting matured in an unconventional way. After studying the batik technique with the artist Toyota in the mid-1960s, it was through her work with children — teaching from 1960 to 1980 at the Montessori-oriented Jardim Escola São Paulo — that she gradually developed a very personal way of seeing Brazil. Her canvases depict samba, June festivals, baianas, open-air markets, the Church of Bonfim, harvests, and urban scenes — dense compositions crowded with figures, in which each element is treated as a full part of the whole. The naïve style shows in the deliberate disproportions (ducks the size of children, giant butterflies and hummingbirds) and in a perspective guided more by her own sense of taste than by academic rules.

Her work, marked by explosive primary colors and a joy that borders on chromatic expressionism, has been shown in solo exhibitions in South Africa, the United States, Germany, and Japan, alongside numerous group shows in Brazil and abroad. Over her career she has also produced an extensive body of prints — serigraphs and fine-art editions widely circulated in the Brazilian popular-art market.
