Clóvis Graciano was a São Paulo–based painter, draftsman, engraver, illustrator, and scenographer who moved to the capital in 1934 and studied with Waldemar da Costa. In 1937 he joined Grupo Santa Helena alongside Francisco Rebolo, Mario Zanini, Aldo Bonadei, and Fulvio Pennacchi, and in 1939 was elected president of the Família Artística Paulista.

After winning a prize at the Salão Nacional de Belas Artes in 1949, he spent two years in Paris studying mural painting and engraving, a discipline that came to dominate his later production in works such as the *Armistício de Iperoig* panel at FAAP (1962). His figurative work centered on the human figure and social themes — workers, migrants, and everyday musical figures — rendered in a humanist, socially engaged register. He directed the Pinacoteca do Estado de São Paulo from 1971 and served as Brazilian cultural attaché in Paris from 1976 to 1978.
