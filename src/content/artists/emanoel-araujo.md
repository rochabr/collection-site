Emanoel Araujo was a Bahian sculptor, printmaker, and painter who trained in graphic composition in Santo Amaro da Purificação and later at the Escola de Belas Artes of the Universidade Federal da Bahia in the 1960s. His practice centered on relief sculpture in wood and metal and on printmaking, fusing a constructivist geometric vocabulary with iconography drawn from Afro-Brazilian and African religious and material culture.

He won the gold medal at the 3rd Florence Print Biennial in 1972 and the APCA awards for best printmaker (1973) and best sculptor (1983), and taught at the City College of New York in 1988. As an institution-builder he directed the Museu de Arte da Bahia (1981–1983), led the Pinacoteca do Estado de São Paulo (1992–2002) — where he expanded the collection and curated landmark shows on Afro-Brazilian art — and in 2004 founded and directed the Museu Afro Brasil in São Paulo, which he ran until his death. He is a foundational figure for the recognition of Afro-Brazilian art history, both through his own work and through the museological infrastructure he created.
