Waldomiro de Deus Souza was born in 1944 in Itagibá, Bahia, and moved to São Paulo in 1959, where he began painting in 1961 using gouache on cardboard. After first selling works at the Viaduto do Chá, he established himself through the 1960s as a self-taught painter of Bahian daily life, popular festivals, religious imagery, and regional folklore such as the *mula-sem-cabeça* and *lobisomem*.

His output is frequently grouped with the leading figures of Brazilian primitivist painting alongside José Antônio da Silva, and his work has circulated through exhibitions in Europe, the United States, and Israel. He is represented in the collection of the Museu Afro Brasil, situating his practice within Afro-Brazilian cultural heritage as well as the broader naïf tradition.
